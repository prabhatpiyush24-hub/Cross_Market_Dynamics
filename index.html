Cross-Market Dynamics: A 10-Year Empirical Study of the Indian Equity Market

Analyzing the relationship between Indian equities (NIFTY 50), global markets, commodities, and volatility.

Abstract & Methodology

Empirical Research Framework

Abstract

This empirical research study investigates the historical behavior of Indian equities (NIFTY 50) in relation to global equity markets (S&P 500, NASDAQ-100), commodities (Brent Crude, Gold), currency (USD/INR), and volatility (India VIX) over a 10-year period.

By employing a strict data alignment methodology to eliminate look-ahead bias, we answer fundamental questions regarding cross-market correlations, the impact of market stress regimes, and the predictive power of global factors on daily Indian market returns.

Research Questions & Hypotheses

  • H1: Previous-session US equity returns have a statistically significant association with subsequent NIFTY returns.
  • H2: Previous-session Brent returns have a statistically significant association with subsequent NIFTY returns.
  • H3: Cross-market relationships differ between low-, normal- and high-volatility regimes.
  • H4: Global-market variables improve out-of-sample NIFTY directional classification relative to a simple baseline.

Data Alignment Methodology

Strictly-Lagged Alignment (No Look-Ahead Bias)

Global markets (US Equities, Commodities) trade in different timezones and often close after the Indian market. To simulate realistic predictive environments, all global variables are aligned to the most recently completed session strictly prior to the Indian market open.

  • Target Anchor: NIFTY 50 daily session.
  • Global Predictors: S&P 500, NASDAQ, Brent, Gold, USD/INR (Lagged).
  • Returns Calculation: Logarithmic/Percentage daily returns derived from the specified pricing field.

Research Limitations

  • Correlation does not establish causation.
  • Daily data may miss intraday relationships.
  • Different market hours and holidays can affect lead/lag results.
  • Market relationships can change over time.
  • Statistical significance does not necessarily imply economic significance.
  • Historical relationships do not guarantee future behaviour.
  • ML performance depends on the selected sample and methodology.
  • Different data providers can produce small differences in historical observations.

End-to-End Methodology & Mathematical Formulas

Transparency

To ensure this research is perfectly transparent and reproducible, the entire data pipeline and modeling process strictly adheres to standard quantitative finance principles. Below is the step-by-step mathematical framework applied from data ingestion to predictive modeling.

1. Data Ingestion & Alignment

10 years of daily historical data was sourced via Yahoo Finance API. To completely eliminate look-ahead bias, global predictors (US Equities, Commodities, Currency) were strictly aligned to the most recently completed session prior to the Indian market open using a backward-looking alignment algorithm. This mathematically guarantees that our models only use information actually known before the Indian bell rang.

2. Mathematical Formulas

Daily Returns (Simple)

Rt = (Pt - Pt-1) / Pt-1

Used to calculate the percentage change between the prices of consecutive trading days.

Annualized Volatility

σann = σdaily × √252

Calculated by taking the standard deviation (σ) of daily returns and multiplying by the square root of 252 (trading days in a year).

Pearson Correlation Coefficient (r)

r = Σ((x - x̄)(y - ȳ)) / √(Σ(x - x̄)²Σ(y - ȳ)²)

Measures the linear correlation between two variables. P-values are calculated using a 2-tailed t-test.

Multiple Linear Regression (OLS)

Y = β0 + β1X1 + ... + ε

Where Y is the NIFTY 50 Daily Return, β are the coefficients, X are the strictly lagged predictors (e.g. S&P 500), and ε is the error term.

Random Forest Classifier (ML)

f(x) = mode(T1(x), ..., TN(x))

An ensemble learning method that constructs a multitude of decision trees at training time and outputs the majority vote of the classes (Positive vs Non-Positive return).

Descriptive Statistics & Distributions

Summary statistics for daily returns across the 10-year research period. These metrics define the "baseline reality" of how these assets behave.

How to read these metrics
  • Annualized Mean Return: The average daily return scaled to a full year (252 days). Note: This differs from historical CAGR.
  • Annualized Volatility: The standard deviation scaled to a full year.
  • Skewness: Shows if returns lean positive or negative. A negative skew means negative returns are more extreme on average than positive returns.
  • Excess Kurtosis: Positive excess kurtosis indicates heavier tails relative to a normal distribution (where excess kurtosis is 0), meaning extreme observations occur more frequently than would be expected under a normal distribution.
  • 5th/95th Pctl: On the worst 5% of days, the asset falls by at least this amount. On the best 5% of days, it rises by this amount.

Key Correlation Findings
  • US and India: Previous-session S&P 500 returns exhibit a statistically significant positive association with subsequent NIFTY returns over the sample period. This relationship indicates statistical association, not causation.
  • USD/INR Relationship: We examine the historical association between USD/INR returns and NIFTY returns. The 10-year lagged correlation is +0.021 with a p-value of 0.275 (Not Statistically Significant).
  • What is a p-value?: A p-value measures how incompatible the observed result is with the null hypothesis. A small p-value provides evidence against the null hypothesis, but it does not represent the probability that the finding is random or that the alternative hypothesis is true.

Machine Learning Experiment

Given the statistically significant linear relationships discovered above, we tested whether Machine Learning algorithms can classify the Direction (Positive vs. Non-Positive) of the next NIFTY 50 session using out-of-sample chronological data (2024-2026).

Understanding ML Metrics
  • Accuracy: Percentage of days the model guessed the direction correctly.
  • ROC-AUC: Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve. A score of 0.500 means no discrimination. Our model achieved an out-of-sample ROC-AUC of approximately 0.597, indicating modest discrimination above the 0.50 benchmark. Further robustness testing is required to determine whether this improvement is stable and economically meaningful.
  • Feature Importance: Displays which variables the Random Forest relied on most heavily for its classifications within the training sample.
