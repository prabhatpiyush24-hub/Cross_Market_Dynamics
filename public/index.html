Cross-Market Dynamics: A 10-Year Empirical Study of the Indian Equity Market

Analyzing the relationship between Indian equities (NIFTY 50), global markets, commodities, and volatility.

Abstract & Methodology

Peer-Review Standard

Abstract

This empirical research study investigates the historical behavior of Indian equities (NIFTY 50) in relation to global equity markets (S&P 500, NASDAQ-100), commodities (Brent Crude, Gold), currency (USD/INR), and volatility (India VIX) over a 10-year period.

By employing a strict data alignment methodology to eliminate look-ahead bias, we answer fundamental questions regarding cross-market correlations, the impact of market stress regimes, and the predictive power of global factors on daily Indian market returns.

Data Alignment Methodology

Strictly-Lagged Alignment (No Look-Ahead Bias)

Global markets (US Equities, Commodities) trade in different timezones and often close after the Indian market. To simulate realistic predictive environments, all global variables are aligned to the most recently completed session strictly prior to the Indian market open.

  • Target Anchor: NIFTY 50 daily session.
  • Global Predictors: S&P 500, NASDAQ, Brent, Gold, USD/INR (Lagged).
  • Returns Calculation: Logarithmic/Percentage daily returns derived from Adjusted Close prices.

End-to-End Methodology & Mathematical Formulas

Transparency

To ensure this research is perfectly transparent and reproducible, the entire data pipeline and modeling process strictly adheres to standard quantitative finance principles. Below is the step-by-step mathematical framework applied from data ingestion to predictive modeling.

1. Data Ingestion & Alignment

10 years of daily historical data was sourced via Yahoo Finance API. To completely eliminate look-ahead bias, global predictors (US Equities, Commodities, Currency) were strictly aligned to the most recently completed session prior to the Indian market open using a backward-looking alignment algorithm. This mathematically guarantees that our models only use information actually known before the Indian bell rang.

2. Mathematical Formulas

Daily Returns (Simple)

Rt = (Pt - Pt-1) / Pt-1

Used to calculate the percentage change between the Adjusted Close prices of consecutive trading days.

Annualized Volatility

σann = σdaily × √252

Calculated by taking the standard deviation (σ) of daily returns and multiplying by the square root of 252 (trading days in a year).

Pearson Correlation Coefficient (r)

r = Σ((x - x̄)(y - ȳ)) / √(Σ(x - x̄)²Σ(y - ȳ)²)

Measures the linear correlation between two variables. P-values are calculated using a 2-tailed t-test.

Multiple Linear Regression (OLS)

Y = β0 + β1X1 + ... + ε

Where Y is the NIFTY 50 Daily Return, β are the coefficients, X are the strictly lagged predictors (e.g. S&P 500), and ε is the error term.

Random Forest Classifier (ML)

f(x) = mode(T1(x), ..., TN(x))

An ensemble learning method that constructs a multitude of decision trees at training time and outputs the majority vote of the classes (Positive vs Non-Positive return).

Descriptive Statistics & Distributions

Summary statistics for daily returns across the 10-year research period. These metrics define the "baseline reality" of how these assets behave.

How to read these metrics
  • Annualized Return: The expected yearly percentage growth if the daily average compounded over 252 trading days.
  • Annualized Volatility: The "risk" or standard deviation. Higher volatility means wilder price swings.
  • Skewness: Shows if returns lean positive or negative. A negative skew (like the S&P 500) means the market grinds up slowly but crashes violently.
  • Kurtosis: Measures "fat tails". A normal bell curve has a kurtosis of 3. Values higher than 3 (like NIFTY's 14+) prove that extreme stock market crashes happen far more often than normal math predicts.
  • 5th/95th Pctl: On the worst 5% of days, the asset falls by at least this amount. On the best 5% of days, it rises by this amount.

Key Correlation Findings
  • The US Leads India: The highly significant (p < 0.01) positive relationship between the previous US session (S&P 500) and the current NIFTY session proves the Indian market heavily follows Wall Street's overnight lead.
  • The USD/INR Myth: While financial media claims Rupee depreciation causes NIFTY crashes, the actual 10-year lagged correlation is +0.021 with a p-value of 0.275 (Not Statistically Significant). The previous day's currency move provides zero predictive value.
  • What is a p-value?: The asterisks (*) denote a p-value < 0.01. This means there is less than a 1% probability that this relationship is just a random coincidence.

Machine Learning Experiment

Given the statistically significant linear relationships discovered above, we tested whether Machine Learning algorithms can predict the Direction (Positive vs. Non-Positive) of the next NIFTY 50 session using only out-of-sample data (2024-2026).

Understanding ML Metrics
  • Accuracy: Percentage of days the model guessed the direction correctly. Anything above 52-53% is considered a massive edge in daily financial markets.
  • ROC-AUC: Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve. A score of 0.500 means random guessing. 1.000 means perfect prediction. Our Random Forest achieves ~0.597, proving it has learned real patterns beyond random chance.
  • Feature Importance: Shows which variables the Random Forest relied on most. Notice how the US Markets (S&P 500 & NASDAQ) contribute over 50% of the predictive power!
